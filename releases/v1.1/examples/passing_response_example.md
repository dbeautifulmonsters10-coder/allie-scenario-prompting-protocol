## Response Text

**S — State the Desired Outcome**

The desired outcome is to reduce average maintenance request response time from 72 hours to under 24 hours across all 120 residential units and three locations, without exceeding a budget of $8,000 and without hiring additional full-time staff, within 90 days.

---

**C — Clarify the Scenario Context**

The company manages 120 residential units across three locations. The current maintenance team consists of four technicians and one part-time scheduler. Response time averages 72 hours. No dedicated maintenance management software is currently in use. The operations director has authorized up to $8,000 for process improvements.

---

**E — Examine Constraints and Risks**

1. **Budget constraint:** $8,000 is sufficient for a mid-tier maintenance management software subscription and a brief onboarding period, but not for significant staffing changes.
2. **Staffing constraint:** Four technicians serving 120 units across three locations may not have spare capacity if demand is uneven across locations. No additional full-time hires are permitted.
3. **Scheduling inefficiency:** Without dedicated software, the part-time scheduler likely relies on manual methods (email, phone, spreadsheets), which introduce delays in request intake, prioritization, and technician assignment.
4. **90-day timeline:** Software onboarding, technician training, and process change typically require 30–60 days. The timeline is achievable but requires prompt action.
5. **Risk:** If response time delays are caused by technician capacity rather than scheduling inefficiency, software alone will not achieve the target.

---

**N — Narrow the Decision Space**

- **Hiring additional staff** is eliminated — explicitly prohibited by constraints.
- **Building a custom software solution** is eliminated — cost and development time far exceed the $8,000 budget and 90-day window.
- **Continuing with manual scheduling** is eliminated — current average of 72 hours indicates the manual process is the primary bottleneck.
- **Outsourcing maintenance work** is eliminated — introduces vendor coordination risk, likely exceeds budget, and removes direct control.

The remaining viable option is implementing a cloud-based maintenance management platform that automates request intake, prioritization, and technician assignment.

---

**A — Analyze with Logic, Reasoning, Coding, or Calculation**

Maintenance management platforms (such as Maintenance Care, Buildium, or AppFolio, cited as illustrative category examples only — actual pricing should be verified) are typically available in the $150–$500/month range for small to mid-size property portfolios. A 12-month contract in the mid-range would cost approximately $1,800–$6,000/year, leaving budget for onboarding, training, and process documentation.

The part-time scheduler's workload would shift from manual intake to platform administration, which is a net efficiency gain without requiring additional hours.

Automated technician dispatch and mobile work order notifications directly address the 72-hour delay caused by communication lag between request intake and technician assignment.

Based on the scenario facts, the primary bottleneck is scheduling and communication, not technician capacity. A software solution directly targets this bottleneck.

*Note: Software pricing above is illustrative. Actual vendor pricing should be confirmed during procurement.*

---

**R — Recommend the Best Single Output**

Implement a cloud-based maintenance management platform. Select a platform appropriate for 120 units across three locations, within the $8,000 budget. Configure automated request intake, prioritization, and technician assignment. Train the four technicians and the part-time scheduler within the first 30 days. Target full adoption within 60 days and measure response time reduction by day 90.

---

**I — Indicate Additional Outcomes Only When Necessary**

If response time remains above 24 hours after software implementation, the secondary option is to restructure the part-time scheduler role into a full-time dispatcher position, which would require a separate budget authorization.

---

**O — Output an Executable Action Plan**

1. **Week 1–2:** Research and select a maintenance management platform within the $8,000 budget. Prioritize platforms with mobile technician apps, automated dispatch, and request tracking dashboards.
2. **Week 2–3:** Execute procurement and initial setup. Configure the platform for three locations and 120 units.
3. **Week 3–4:** Train the part-time scheduler on platform administration and request intake.
4. **Week 4–5:** Train the four technicians on the mobile app and work order workflow.
5. **Week 5–8:** Run parallel operations — track response times in the platform while completing the transition from manual processes.
6. **Week 8:** Decommission manual scheduling processes.
7. **Day 90:** Measure average response time. Success criterion: average response time is 24 hours or below. If target is not met, conduct root cause analysis.
8. **Ongoing:** Review response time metrics monthly and adjust dispatch rules or scheduling as needed.
